# 6.1 Timing and Interrupt Delivery

## Boundary and result

6.1 covers host timer ticks, heartbeat scheduling, event wait/wake/idle
transition, IRQ/EOI hook delivery, and worker retirement at the host boundary.
It excludes guest/device/ICA algorithms and WOW-only scheduler cooperation.
Four fixed family contracts are frozen and statically closed; no provider,
compiled ABI, concurrency or runtime behavior is validated.

## Crucial qualifications

Heartbeat delivery after timer/RTC work does not promise wall-clock cadence,
no skipped intervals, or eventual completion. `HostIdle` is a wake notification,
not a drain guarantee. An EOI callback may convey a cancellation value, but
unregistration does not establish that no invocation is in flight. A worker's
own thread-handle close and exit does not prove that its caller joined; one
historical alertable wait ignores its result. These are source facts that prevent
false safety claims in a modern adapter.

## Minimum-diff direction

Preserve timer/event/IRQ owner separation, callback order and cancellation
markers. Add a bounded, explicit quiescence/lifetime policy only where modern
providers require one. Do not turn a wake pulse into a queue-drain guarantee or
rebuild device/CPU scheduling. Input handoffs go to 5.2, display ordering to
5.3, device callbacks to 6.3, lifecycle to 2.3, and implementation validation
to Part 8.
