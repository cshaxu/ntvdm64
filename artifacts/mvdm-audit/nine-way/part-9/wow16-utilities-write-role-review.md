# Part 9: Win16 Write, spooler and debugging assets

The 119 MVDM identities for this logical part are individually captured in `mvdm-identity-observations.json`; reproduce with `mvdm-identity-observer.py`. The Write subset includes its C editing, document, printing and formatting implementation, assembly support, international/Kanji data, bitmap controls, definition file, source/build notes, and two retained executable filenames. `write/readme.txt` describes historical toolchain and localization variants; it explicitly cautions against treating all makefiles and localization sources as equivalent. The current retained `WRITE.EXE` and `write16.exe` have the same SHA-256 `08EE…0B`; only `write16.exe` was located in the OpenNT-4.5 original tree, so exact provenance needs that edition rather than a guessed local rebuild.

`guest-package-observer.py` shows packaged `WINNT/WRITE.EXE` has a different SHA-256 `EB44…87A` from the retained mirror executables. It does not establish why they differ or whether either binary was generated by this source. Do not rebuild or replace a guest based on the filename. Behavioral evidence and WRI scenarios must be associated with the actual executed image.

`winspool/makefile` compiles the owned `winspool.asm` and links its driver/application with `winspool.def` and `WINSPOOL.RES`; the packaged `SYSTEM32/WINSPOOL.EXE` is byte-identical to the retained executable. This proves package identity, not source-to-binary provenance or working print integration. `wowdeb/makefile` selects a separate debug helper and references KERNEL.511; the inspected ZIP lacks `SYSTEM32/WOWDEB.EXE`. `winsock/WINSOCK.RES` is a resource input, not itself proof of active socket behavior or of the deployed Winsock binary's origin.

Do not discard guest capability because its source is outside host compilation. No guest build, binary execution, WRI, printing or socket test was performed here; retain roles and leave provenance and behavior pending where unsupported.
