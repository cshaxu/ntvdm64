# SOL game-thread instruction sampling

The current ten-image candidate completed one Options interaction with the test-only probe sampling the PID-bound game-window thread approximately every 25 ms while waiting for open/close. Each sample suspends only that owned thread, captures its x86 PC, and immediately resumes it; failed resume makes the probe fail so the owned-test cleanup can terminate its stage. The run returned success and yielded 115 samples. The process CPU-time observation is separate. Module bases were captured from the owned worker, and PCs were mapped to nearest symbols in the selected worker/WOW32 map; map hashes are in analysis.json. Probe source SHA-256 follows below.

During open, the most frequent observed locations included win32u (15), ntdll (14), session_thread_current (9), phyR (7), and copy_window_observation (4). During close, win32u (7), phyR (5), ntdll (4), session_thread_current (3), xtrn2phy (3) and qsort (3) appeared. These are counts of instruction samples, not exact CPU shares, not stacks or per-function call counts. Sampling perturbs scheduling, only one thread is observed, module attribution for system DLLs has no function symbols, and nearest-symbol lookup can misattribute optimized or folded code. They suggest looking at guest-memory mapping and repeated session lookup alongside native calls; they do not prove a single root cause or justify changing 486 code.
